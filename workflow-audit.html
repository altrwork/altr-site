Workflow audit: map the process before AI touches it

Before anything is built we map one repeated process end to end: inputs, decisions, tools, handoffs, review points, and failure modes. The output is a decision rather than a proposal. We run audits on site across Tampa Bay, where we are based, and over video for teams anywhere in the US.

Four things we document.

The real path

How the work actually moves, including the steps that live in someone's head, the spreadsheet nobody mentions, and the exceptions that break the documented process.

Where the drag is

Rework, waiting, duplicate entry, and handoffs that stall. We look for repeated work with clear inputs, useful outputs, review points, and measurable drag.

The control path

Permissions, human validation, fallback behavior, logs, and ownership. We define these before any system goes live, not after.

The honest recommendation

Enable, automate, or leave it alone, with the reasoning attached. Sometimes the answer is a training session and a better prompt, not a build.

Where audits usually lead.

An audit ends in one of three places, and all three are acceptable outcomes. A fourth situation is not an outcome but a warning that the mapping will take longer.

The team needs skill, not software.

The workflow is fine; the people doing it have not been shown how AI fits inside it. That becomes an enablement workshop using their own examples.

The repeated part should be a system.

The work has clear inputs, a real review point, and enough volume to justify a build. That becomes a workflow automation or custom agent, then a phased rollout.

The process is not ready.

The inputs are inconsistent, the owner is unclear, or the volume does not justify the maintenance. We say so. A build on top of that produces a system nobody trusts.

Nobody can explain the process yet.

When the workflow spans teams and no single person sees all of it, the mapping takes longer, because every handoff between those teams has to be walked through with the people on both sides of it.

Questions teams ask before an audit.

What is a workflow audit?

A workflow audit is a structured pass over one repeated business process that documents its real inputs, decisions, tools, handoffs, review points, and failure modes. The output is a decision: enable the team to do the work better with AI, automate the repeated parts, or leave the process alone.

Why not skip discovery and start building?

Because automating a process nobody can explain is the quickest way to a system nobody trusts. If the team cannot say where judgment belongs and what good output looks like, an automation will only produce wrong answers faster. The audit is the cheapest place to find that out.

What do we get at the end?

A map of the workflow as it actually runs, the points where time and rework are lost, a recommendation with the reasoning behind it, and the control path any future system would need: permissions, human validation, fallback behavior, logging, and ownership.

What makes a workflow a good candidate?

Repeated work with clear inputs, useful outputs, a natural review point, and measurable drag. Processes that run weekly or daily, have an obvious owner, and produce something a person currently checks by hand are the strongest candidates.

What should we prepare?

Three or four real examples of the work: what came in, what went out, and anything that went wrong. Bring the person who does the work, not only the person who manages it, and a list of the tools it touches. Screenshots are fine.

Pick the process that annoys your team most.

That is usually the right one to look at first. Bring it to the call and we will map it. No obligation to build anything.
