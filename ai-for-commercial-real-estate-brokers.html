AI for commercial real estate brokers

Alex BrittonSeptember 28, 2026

Most brokers start by asking AI to write an email. The useful work is upstream: finding the owner, assembling comps, and keeping the pipeline clean.

Short answer

Commercial real estate brokers use AI to find and research owners from public records, assemble broker opinions of value and offering memorandum drafts, pull and screen comparable sales, clean up CRM records, send scheduled market updates, and draft follow-up. The broker keeps pricing, advice to clients, and anything sent under their name without review.

Where does AI save a broker the most time?

On repeated, rule-bound work sourced from records you are allowed to use: the research and paperwork around a deal, not the deal.

How do brokers use AI to prospect owners?

By asking the county roll questions the county website cannot answer. Assessors publish the owner of record, tax-bill mailing address, use class, building size, assessed value and at least the last sale for every parcel. In a database Claude can query, that becomes one question: industrial buildings of 20,000 to 60,000 square feet in one county, held more than ten years, owned by an LLC with an out-of-state mailing address.

Next comes the person behind the LLC. State corporate registries list a registered agent and often managers or officers, so Claude can go from the name on the deed to the names on the filing and to everything else those names own. Some filings name only a lawyer, so treat an unmatched name as unknown, not as a finding. Our county records connector does this across Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco and Polk.

Stick to sources you are allowed to automate. Can Claude connect to CoStar? covers why licensed platforms are off the list.

How does AI help with BOVs, OMs and comps?

It assembles the evidence and drafts the prose. You supply the judgment. A broker opinion of value is mostly gathering: parcel record, sale history, screened comps, and a layout in the firm's style. mortr, our CRM for CRE teams, generates that document in your branding from county records and leaves the conclusion to you.

An OM works the same way: from a rent roll, photos and your notes, Claude drafts the property, location and tenancy sections. Rewrite the parts that sell. For comps, the value is in the screening: drop $100 deeds and multi-parcel portfolio sales, and state the file date so a thin recent month is not read as a slowdown. Write those rules once in a Skill.

How can AI keep a CRM, market updates and follow-up current?

By running the same small checks every week without anyone remembering to.

  • CRM hygiene. Connected to the CRM, a model can list contacts with no activity in 90 days, deals with no next step, and duplicate companies spelled three ways. The broker decides what to merge. See Claude in CRE: Connectors.
  • Market updates. A scheduled task can pull the week's recorded sales in a submarket each Monday and draft a short summary. Read it before it goes anywhere.
  • Follow-up. After a meeting, the model drafts the follow-up email and the CRM note from your notes. It drafts. You send.

For rent rolls and models, see Claude Cowork for CRE.

Where do personal AI agents like Meta's Muse fit?

On the broker's own marketing, not on firm data. A personal agent works for one person across that person's accounts. The useful job: turning a checked weekly submarket summary or closed-deal announcement into posts for accounts and groups you already work. Meta does not document direct posting to Facebook or Instagram as of September 2026, so test it first. Keep client files, owner lists and licensed data out unless your firm has approved it. Personal AI agents for business covers the category.

What should a broker not hand to AI?

Anything where being wrong costs a client or breaks a rule.

  • The value conclusion and the pricing recommendation. They carry your license and your name.
  • Advice to a client about whether to sell, what to accept, or how to structure a deal.
  • Licensed data your subscription does not allow in AI tools. Read the terms before pasting a screen into a chat.
  • Confidential client material in a consumer account without the data controls your firm has approved.
  • Unreviewed sends. Nothing goes to a client, a counterparty or a mailing list without a person reading it.
  • Legal, tax and environmental conclusions, which belong to the professionals who sign for them.

What does a 30-day AI pilot look like for a brokerage?

One workflow, one owner, one measure, real deals, four weeks.

  1. Week 1: choose and map. Pick the workflow that is most repeated, often owner research or BOV assembly. Write down every step as it is done today and how long it takes.
  2. Week 2: build it by hand. Run the workflow with AI on three live properties. Save the prompts that worked. Note every correction you made.
  3. Week 3: make it repeatable. Turn the prompts and corrections into a Skill or a checklist, connect the sources you are allowed to automate, and have a second person run it.
  4. Week 4: measure and decide. Compare time per property and error count against week one. Keep it, change it, or drop it.

A pilot that ends in "drop it" is still a result. A pilot that never names its measure is not.

A starting prompt for week 2:

For the parcel at [address], list the owner of record, mailing address,
use code, building area, year built, just value and every recorded sale.
Mark any sale under $1,000 or any deed that conveyed more than one parcel.
Then list qualified sales of the same use code within [size band] in the
last 24 months, and state the date of the sales file you used.

Common questions

Can AI replace a commercial real estate broker?

No. AI can gather records, screen comparable sales and draft documents, but pricing, client advice, negotiation and the relationship remain the broker's work. It shortens the research and paperwork around a deal, not the deal.

What is the best first AI use case for a CRE broker?

Usually owner research or BOV assembly. Both repeat on every deal, draw on public records a broker is allowed to automate, and have an output that is easy to check against the county record.

Is it safe to put client information into AI tools?

Only in accounts with data controls your firm has approved. Use a business or team plan with the retention and training settings you have reviewed, and keep confidential material out of personal consumer accounts.

Can AI pull comps from CoStar?

Not in a way the terms allow. CoStar's public terms restrict sharing access with AI agents, scraping, and using its data as input to generative AI. Public records such as county sales files are the source a broker can automate.
