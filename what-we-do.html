Questions people ask

Why are there no prices on this page?

Because the first thing we sell is four hours, and what it costs depends on how far along you are and where you are. We would rather quote you on a call than publish a number we then explain away. Ask and you get it straight, in writing, before you commit to anything.

How does the credit work?

The four-hour session is paid. If you go on to run a workshop for the wider team, what you paid for the session comes off the workshop fee. If you stop after the session, you keep the workflow map and the prompts and owe nothing further.

Working session or team workshop?

Both, in that order. The session is hands-on with the one to four people who own a process, and it produces the map. The workshop takes that map to the wider team so nobody starts cold. If you only ever do one, do the session.

Why is build pricing not listed?

A build costs what its scope costs, and scope is unknown until the workflow is mapped. Quoting a number before that means padding it or walking it back later. The session produces the map; the build gets quoted from it as one fixed fee against written scope.

What is the difference between enablement and a build?

Enablement leaves your team prompts and workflows they run by hand, in tools they already have. A build is software engineering: multi-step agent orchestrations that act on their own, MCP servers wired into your systems, deployed and monitored. Nobody has to sit there and drive it.

Can we start with the retainer?

No. Advisory hours only pay for themselves once we know how your work actually moves, and that is what the session produces. Buying the retainer first means paying us to guess, which is why it is third on this page.

Can I use retainer hours to get something built?

No, and that is deliberate. Ten hours a month is advisory time: reviewing what your team built, keeping a deployed system healthy, or steering a decision before it turns into rework. Real engineering gets scoped as a build so you get one number and we carry the risk of it running long.

What is included in a quote?

A quote covers the consulting time it names. No separate charges for calls, documentation, or travel within Tampa Bay.

Do you require a long-term contract?

No. Builds are discrete pieces of work with a defined end, and the retainer runs month to month if you want. Longer terms exist because they cost less, not because we require them.

What if we are smaller than this?

Start with the session. Small teams are the easiest case, not the hardest, because the person who owns the process is already in the room. It usually reveals that the first useful step is smaller and cheaper than expected.
