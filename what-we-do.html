Questions people ask about the numbers

Working session or team workshop?

Depth or breadth, at the same price. Pick the working session when one process matters most and the people who own it can be in a room; you get a written map of that workflow. Pick the team workshop when the goal is getting a whole group off zero. If you want both, run the sessions first and the workshop second, built on what the sessions found.

We are only two people. Is it cheaper?

No, and it is also not the same product. Two people is a working session: we go deeper, and you leave with a map of your process rather than a room full of prompts. You are not buying seats, so there are no unused ones to discount.

Why is build pricing not listed?

A build costs what its scope costs, and scope is unknown until the workflow is mapped. Quoting a number before that means padding it or walking it back later. Enablement produces the map; the build gets quoted from it as one fixed fee against written scope.

What is the difference between enablement and a build?

Enablement leaves your team prompts and workflows they run by hand, in tools they already have. A build is software engineering: multi-step agent orchestrations that act on their own, MCP servers wired into your systems, deployed and monitored. Nobody has to sit there and drive it.

Can we start with the retainer?

No. Advisory hours only pay for themselves once we know how your work actually moves, and that is what enablement produces. Buying the retainer first means paying us to guess. It is the cheapest line on this page and the wrong place to start, which is why it is third.

Can I use retainer hours to get something built?

No, and that is deliberate. Ten hours a month is advisory time: reviewing what your team built, keeping a deployed system healthy, or steering a decision before it turns into rework. Real engineering gets scoped as a build so you get one number and we carry the risk of it running long.

What is included in a quote?

A quote covers the consulting time it names. No separate charges for calls, documentation, or travel within Tampa Bay.

Do you require a long-term contract?

No. Builds are discrete pieces of work with a defined end, and the retainer runs month to month if you want. The three and six month terms exist because they cost less, not because we require them.

What if we are smaller than this?

Start with a working session. Small teams are the easiest case, not the hardest, because the person who owns the process is already in the room. It usually reveals that the first useful step is smaller and cheaper than expected.

Bring one workflow and we will price it.

Tell us the process your team keeps doing by hand. You will get a straight answer on whether it needs training, a build, or nothing at all.
