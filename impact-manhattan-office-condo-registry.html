Commercial real estate

Every Manhattan office condo, from public record.

A brokerage asked us to bring a Tampa property-research tool to Manhattan. The research said no. Florida requires an LLC to name its officers in the annual report, and the state publishes those officers in a free bulk file, so an address there resolves all the way to a named human. New York closes every equivalent path: the state's corporate data carries no members or managers, the LLC Transparency Act covers only foreign LLCs behind a law-enforcement database, and the permit filings that do collect an owner email strip it before publication.

So we did not port the tool. We built the thing New York's records can actually support: a registry of every office condominium in Manhattan, assembled from ACRIS deeds, mortgages and satisfactions, the Digital Tax Map, and the Department of Finance assessment roll. 639 buildings, 4,507 units, 38,326 recorded documents, 1972 to 2026.

It ships as a Claude Desktop extension. The database travels inside it, so there is no Python to install, no server to run, and no configuration file to edit. A broker installs one file and starts asking questions in plain language.

What the records give you, and what they do not.

Public record is not a cheaper CoStar. It is a different set of facts, and the useful ones are further along than people expect.

Owners you can reach

3,321 units, 73.7% of the registry, have a mailable owner: a deed address confirmed against the assessment roll rather than assumed. 478 units are held by a named institution or person rather than an LLC.

No phone numbers, no email

Neither exists in New York public record, so the registry does not claim them. Nor does it carry lease or rent comparables, which sit behind private databases and cannot be legally sourced. It covers investment sales, and says so.

We tested it against a number someone had already published.

A market total for the second half of 2025, compiled by hand: $93.0M across 21 deals at $447 per rentable square foot.

What the registry computed

$92,935,954 across 19 deals at $451 per rentable square foot. The volume lands within 0.07% of the published figure, a difference of $64,046 on $93M, from free records and no manual compilation.

Why that is not proof yet

Three filter parameters were fitted to three published statistics, which makes this calibration rather than independent validation. The honest test is a period the filters have never seen, run live. We would rather say that than let the number stand on its own.

Your market may have more in its public record than you think.

Every county and every state publishes a different set of facts. We find out what yours supports before anyone promises a product, and we say so when the answer is that it does not.
