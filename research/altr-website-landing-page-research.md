# altr Website and Landing Page Research

Generated: 2026-06-14  
Business context: altr is a Tampa-based AI deployment studio offering practical AI enablement, workflow automation, custom agents, coding-agent systems, and forward-deployed engineering for small and growing teams.

## Executive Summary

The most effective website for altr should not behave like a generic AI agency landing page. The market is crowded with claims about agents, automation, and transformation, while buyers are increasingly self-directed, conservative, and skeptical of AI hype. The site should make altr feel like a practical implementation partner: clear about the workflows it improves, specific about how engagements work, visible about proof, and explicit about governance, ownership, handoffs, and human review.

The current site already has the right strategic direction: "AI deployment studio," "real workflows," "permissions and review paths," "embed-enable-deploy," and impact studies. The main opportunity is to make the homepage more instantly concrete: who it helps, which workflows are good fits, what the buyer gets, what happens in the first 30-60 days, and what proof exists from past work.

## Research Questions

1. How do B2B buyers evaluate service providers before contacting sales?
2. What landing-page structure works for complex, high-trust B2B services?
3. What specific trust barriers exist for AI agents and automation?
4. What proof patterns are strongest for AI deployment and workflow automation businesses?
5. What should altr change on its site based on this evidence?

## Key Findings

### 1. The homepage must answer "what do you do?" faster and more concretely.

Nielsen Norman Group's homepage guidance says a homepage should clearly communicate who the company is, what it does, reveal content through examples, prompt actions, and stay simple. Their separate page-attention research says users often leave pages within 10-20 seconds unless the value proposition is clear quickly.

For altr, "altr the way you work" is strong as a brand line, but it should be supported immediately by a plain-English operational promise. The current hero copy is accurate but long and abstract. A first-time buyer should quickly understand:

- altr helps teams identify and deploy practical AI workflows.
- altr builds agents and automations around existing tools, permissions, and review paths.
- altr is for teams that need working systems, not AI demos.
- the next step is a short intro or workflow audit.

Source: [Nielsen Norman Group, Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/) and [Nielsen Norman Group, How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)

### 2. Above-the-fold content should include proof or a visible hint of proof.

NN/g's fold research found that the top of the page still strongly affects whether users continue. Users scroll when the visible page gives them reason to expect value below. This matters for altr because the first screen should not rely only on positioning and brand feel. It should expose at least one concrete proof element:

- a compact client logo row,
- one quantified workflow result,
- a line such as "Recent work: spARK Labs, Fishin Prints, BeBrief,"
- or a small "see impact studies" path.

Source: [Nielsen Norman Group, The Fold Manifesto](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/page-fold-manifesto/)

### 3. B2B buyers research heavily before they talk to vendors.

6sense's 2025 Buyer Experience Report says buyers can execute about two-thirds of the buying journey, including selecting likely winning vendors, before engaging sellers. Demand Gen Report's summary of 6sense's earlier research reported that 81% of buyers had a preferred vendor at first contact and 85% had already established purchase requirements.

The implication for altr: the site has to sell before the intro call. It cannot depend on the call to explain the offer. It needs enough self-serve substance for a founder, operator, or department lead to internally justify "this is worth a conversation."

Source: [6sense, B2B Buyer Experience Report 2025](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/) and [Demand Gen Report on 6sense buyer research](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/80-of-b2b-buyers-initiate-first-contact-once-theyre-70-through-their-buying-journey/48394/)

### 4. Buyers want self-service for general learning, but human guidance for fit.

Gartner's 2025 B2B sales survey found buyers prefer digital self-service for general information and learning, but prefer seller input when assessing fit for their specific company. Gartner's 2026 survey found 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience and 45% used AI during a recent purchase. It also emphasized "value clarity," meaning buyers need confidence about how a solution improves outcomes in their specific role and context.

For altr, this argues for a hybrid conversion path:

- self-serve pages that explain use cases, process, ownership, and risks,
- a call-to-action framed around fit diagnosis, not a generic sales call,
- a "what happens next" section that makes the intro feel useful even if no project follows.

Source: [Gartner, 2025 B2B buyer survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience) and [Gartner, 2026 B2B buyer survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)

### 5. AI buyers are worried about failed pilots, unclear ROI, governance, and hype.

McKinsey's 2025 State of AI research says AI value correlates with practices such as embedding AI into business processes, tracking KPIs, and defining when human validation is required. Deloitte's 2026 State of AI report says worker AI access rose in 2025, but the skills gap is the biggest barrier to integration and only one in five companies has mature governance for autonomous AI agents. IBM's 2025 breach report highlights the AI oversight gap, including lack of access controls and governance policies among organizations with AI-related incidents.

Gartner also predicts more agent adoption in enterprise apps, but warns that over 40% of agentic AI projects may be canceled by the end of 2027 due to costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls.

For altr, this is a positioning advantage. The site should make "workflow fit," "human validation," "permissions," "review paths," "handoff," and "governance" highly visible. That language is not boring; it is what makes the buyer feel the team understands the real problem.

Sources: [McKinsey, The State of AI 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai), [Deloitte, State of AI in the Enterprise 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html), [IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach), [Gartner agentic AI cancellation prediction](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027), and [Gartner task-specific AI agents prediction](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025)

### 6. Case studies should lead with operational before/after, not implementation detail.

AI automation and agent companies that show results tend to make the outcome concrete: faster processing, hours saved, fewer manual steps, claims automated, candidate screening time reduced, provisioning time cut, or customer interactions handled. UiPath and Beam AI both present case studies around workflow outcomes and quantified improvements rather than only technology.

altr already has impact studies. The homepage should surface them more forcefully with a repeatable structure:

- Workflow: what process was manual or slow?
- Intervention: what AI-assisted system was deployed?
- Human control: where review or approval stayed in the loop?
- Result: time saved, cycle shortened, quality improved, or new capability unlocked.
- Asset: video, screenshot, workflow map, or customer quote.

Sources: [UiPath automation case studies](https://www.uipath.com/resources/automation-case-studies) and [Beam AI case studies](https://beam.ai/resources/case-studies)

### 7. The best competitive pages make the engagement model tangible.

Digital Scientists' forward-deployed AI engineering page is directly relevant. It positions the work as moving AI from pilot to production workflow, says the team owns deployment outcomes, distinguishes itself from consulting and staff augmentation, names the steps, and answers questions about engagement length and ownership. AutomateNexus' Tampa page is less premium but very concrete: local target, use cases, pricing, time-to-live, ownership, audit, process, FAQs, and nearby service-area content.

altr's current page is more tasteful and differentiated, but the site could borrow the useful clarity:

- "Who this is for"
- "Best-fit workflows"
- "What we build"
- "How long it takes"
- "What you own afterward"
- "What we need from your team"
- "When we will say this is not a fit"

Sources: [Digital Scientists, Forward Deployed AI Engineering](https://digitalscientists.com/services/forward-deployed-ai-engineering/) and [AutomateNexus Tampa AI automation page](https://automatenexus.com/locations/florida/tampa)

### 8. Local trust matters because altr is Tampa-positioned.

Google's LocalBusiness structured data guidance supports giving search engines business context such as location, business details, departments, and reviews. BrightLocal's 2025 review research shows consumers use more than traditional review platforms for local business research, including local news and video content.

For altr, local positioning should not mean "small local vendor." It should mean "reachable operating partner in Tampa Bay with remote capability." The site should support local trust with:

- Tampa/Tampa Bay in title and metadata, already present,
- LocalBusiness schema, already present,
- visible local presence in page copy,
- local case studies or events,
- Google Business Profile and LinkedIn consistency,
- short videos or event recaps if practical.

Sources: [Google Search Central, LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) and [BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)

### 9. Site speed and form friction matter for lead generation.

Google and Deloitte's "Milliseconds Make Millions" study found speed improvements affected engagement and lead-generation journeys, including a measured uplift from first form step to submission after a 0.1 second improvement in one lead-gen context. The broader point is that a high-trust B2B site should not bury conversion under heavy media, scripts, or unclear forms.

For altr:

- keep the visual system warm and premium but lightweight,
- avoid unnecessary animation or large media in the first render,
- make the booking/intake path short,
- show what happens after form submission,
- collect enough context to qualify without making the user write a proposal.

Source: [Think with Google, Milliseconds Make Millions](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9757/Milliseconds_Make_Millions_report_hQYAbZJ.pdf)

## Modern Tech Website Design References

The redesign should borrow patterns from current high-end tech sites without copying their visual systems directly.

### Vercel

Vercel leads with a short claim, supporting line, product categories, proof from recognizable customers, and performance-oriented outcomes. Its homepage uses the site itself as a signal of speed and technical confidence. For altr, the useful pattern is not the black-and-white Vercel brand, but the hierarchy: clear claim, immediate proof, and concrete product/application paths.

Source: [Vercel homepage](https://vercel.com/) and [Metabrand analysis of Vercel](https://www.metabrand.digital/learn/tech-website-design-best-examples)

### Linear

Linear's homepage is product-forward and precise. It uses restrained copy, dark/high-contrast surfaces, and language around workflows shared by humans and AI agents. For altr, the relevant lesson is that motion and interface visuals should demonstrate the core promise. If the promise is reliable AI inside workflows, the hero visual should look like a workflow/control surface, not a generic stock-style brand image.

Source: [Linear homepage](https://linear.app/) and [Metabrand analysis of Linear](https://www.metabrand.digital/learn/tech-website-design-best-examples)

### Cursor

Cursor makes the product concept tangible by showing an interface-like demo and by making the core promise extremely simple: agents turn ideas into code while humans stay focused on decisions. For altr, the analogous move is to show the deployment path: intake, agent draft, human review, and system handoff.

Source: [Cursor homepage](https://cursor.com/)

### Anthropic

Anthropic is a useful counterweight in the AI category because it uses restraint, clarity, and measured language instead of speculative AI visuals. For altr, this supports a premium, grounded direction: strong typography, practical claims, and explicit control/governance language.

Source: [Metabrand analysis of Anthropic](https://www.metabrand.digital/learn/tech-website-design-best-examples)

### Figma's 2026 Trend Guidance

Figma's 2026 trend guide calls out bold typography, dark mode, motion, accessibility, sustainable/lean design, and progressive lead nurturing. For altr, the useful subset is bold type, lightweight interface-like visuals, a clean pill navigation pattern, and forms/CTAs that collect context progressively rather than overwhelming visitors.

Source: [Figma Web Design Trends 2026](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)

### Applied Design Direction for altr

The homepage should feel like a refined operating system for work: warm but sharper, text-led, interface-led, and proof-led. The new direction should:

- Remove the hero photo/image and replace it with a custom workflow console.
- Use a floating pill navbar to feel more current and less template-like.
- Put proof near the top with client logos and recent-work language.
- Use a dense but readable grid for workflow examples.
- Bring impact studies onto the homepage as modern case cards.
- Make governance and human review visually prominent.
- Keep the brand's tactile warmth, but add charcoal, moss, oxide, and blue-gray accents so the page no longer reads as a single beige/taupe theme.

## Dark Mode and Centered Hero Revision

After reviewing the page in context, the strongest direction is darker, simpler, and more centered. The earlier workflow-console hero solved the "no stock image" problem, but it still read like an AI-generated SaaS mockup. For altr, that is the wrong signal. The company is selling practical judgment and implementation taste, so the hero should be confident enough to be mostly copy, proof, and a single clear action.

Dark mode is appropriate for this revision, but not because dark mode is automatically better. Nielsen Norman Group notes that dark mode is popular but should not take priority over basic usability, readability, and task clarity. Metabrand's tech-site analysis makes the same point from a brand direction angle: the best dark tech sites use darkness purposefully, while the weakest use it because it looks modern. For altr, dark mode is justified if it communicates focus, technical seriousness, and restraint while keeping copy highly readable.

Sources: [NN/g Dark Mode Best Practices](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues/), [Metabrand Tech Website Design Examples](https://www.metabrand.digital/learn/tech-website-design-best-examples)

The revised homepage direction should:

- Keep the navbar as the one intentional pill-shaped object.
- Remove generic pill callouts from proof, tags, stats, and cards.
- Remove the AI-coded hero image/interface mockup.
- Center the first viewport around the headline, short copy, proof line, and CTA.
- Use a dark charcoal background with warm ivory type and restrained copper/sage/steel accents.
- Use square or lightly rounded cards instead of pill-heavy SaaS UI.
- Preserve proof and workflow specificity below the hero.

### 1. Hero: clear promise, proof, one primary action

Recommended content:

- Kicker: "AI deployment studio / Tampa + remote"
- Headline: keep "altr the way you work" if the supporting line is very concrete.
- Supporting line: "We help teams turn repeated work into AI-assisted workflows, agents, and automations that fit their tools, permissions, and review paths."
- Proof micro-line: "Recent work with spARK Labs, Fishin Prints, and BeBrief."
- CTA: "Book a workflow intro"
- Secondary CTA: "See impact studies"

### 2. The problem: the gap between AI demos and daily operations

Use this to name the buyer's pain:

- AI tools are everywhere.
- The hard part is deciding where they belong.
- Useful systems need context, permissions, review paths, and adoption.
- altr handles the implementation layer.

### 3. Best-fit workflows

Create a scannable list of workflows, not service abstractions:

- intake and request routing,
- document generation and review,
- internal research and briefing,
- customer follow-up,
- recurring operations reports,
- expense/event/request workflows,
- coding-agent loops for product teams,
- image/content production workflows where applicable.

### 4. Engagement model

Show the operating shape:

- Embed: map workflow, tools, permissions, approval path.
- Enable: workshops, demos, prompts, team habits.
- Deploy: agent, automation, integration, review harness, documentation.
- Transfer: ownership, operating notes, training, iteration plan.

### 5. Proof section

Turn impact studies into conversion assets:

- spARK Labs: expenses and event requests with Claude Cowork.
- Fishin Prints: AI image workflow from customer catch photos to production brief.
- BeBrief: coding-agent system for product development.

Each card should include a workflow category and a concrete before/after line.

### 6. Trust and governance

Add a short "How we keep AI useful and controlled" section:

- human review where judgment matters,
- clear permissions and tool boundaries,
- logs and handoff notes,
- fallback paths,
- no lock-in where possible,
- client owns workflows and documentation.

### 7. FAQ / objection handling

Answer the questions likely blocking a conversation:

- What kind of workflow is a good fit?
- Do we need clean data first?
- Is this a workshop, consulting, or build engagement?
- How long does the first engagement take?
- Who owns the work afterward?
- What tools do you work with?
- How do you handle sensitive data?
- What if the best answer is not an agent?

### 8. Conversion section

The CTA should feel diagnostic:

- "Bring us one workflow your team keeps repeating."
- "In the intro, we will identify whether it needs enablement, automation, a custom agent, or nothing yet."
- "If there is no fit, we will say so."

## Positioning Recommendation

The strongest positioning for altr:

> altr is the practical AI deployment studio for teams that want AI inside real work, not stranded in demos.

Supporting pillars:

- Embedded: close to the actual workflow.
- Practical: workshops, demos, and systems built around existing tools.
- Controlled: permissions, review paths, and human validation.
- Owned: clients keep the workflows, documentation, and operating knowledge.
- Local but capable: Tampa-based, remote-friendly.

Avoid leading with:

- vague "transform your business" language,
- generic chatbot/AI automation agency framing,
- agent hype without constraints,
- too many service categories before examples.
