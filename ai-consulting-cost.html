How much does AI consulting cost? It depends on things you can name before the first call.

Most firms do not publish a rate card, including us. You can still know how the work is priced, what moves the number, and which costs never reach the consultant's invoice.

Short answer

AI consulting is usually sold one of five ways: hourly, fixed-fee project, monthly retainer, subscription or outcome-based, and workshop day-rate. The number depends on team size, how many workflows are in scope, how many systems need connecting, how ready your data is, security review, and who maintains the result. Model usage, seats and hosting are billed separately by vendors.

How do AI consultants price their work?

Many firms combine these models by phase. Each moves risk between you and the consultant in a different direction, and the right one depends on how well the work can be described before it starts.

A pattern that works: a short, bounded engagement to find the work, a fixed fee for anything that needs building, then optional ongoing help.

What drives the price up or down?

Six things explain most of the gap between a small quote and a large one. You can answer most before you talk to anyone.

  • Team size. Four people who own one process is a different job from forty.
  • Number of workflows. Five processes across three departments is five problems that rarely share a solution.
  • Systems to integrate. Work inside a chat window or a folder of files is cheap. Each live system the AI reads from or writes to (a CRM, accounting, property management) adds sign-in, permissions and testing.
  • Data readiness. Consistent files in one place go quickly. Inboxes, scanned PDFs and a spreadsheet only one person understands can make cleanup the largest line item.
  • Security review. Regulated industries, confidentiality clauses and vendor questionnaires add time. Put it in the estimate.
  • Who maintains it. A prompt library run by hand needs little upkeep. A deployed agent that calls live systems needs monitoring, updates and a named owner.

What costs sit outside the consultant's fee?

Vendors usually bill seats and model usage directly. Hosting sits with the vendor or inside a consultant's monthly fee. Costs grow with usage, so ask for an estimate at the volume you expect in a year, not only at launch.

  • Seats. Per-user subscriptions to an assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT on a business plan. Often the largest recurring line for a small team.
  • Model or API usage. Automations and agents that call a model through an API are metered by usage. Thousands of documents a day is a real budget line.
  • Hosting. A deployed agent runs somewhere. It is a bill in your name or inside a monthly fee that says what it covers.
  • Automation platforms and data. Tools such as Zapier or Make meter usage on their own plans, and licensed data sources have their own terms, some of which restrict AI use entirely.

A good proposal lists each one, says who pays whom, and puts the accounts in your name.

What are the red flags in an AI consulting quote?

The warning signs are about scope and ownership, not the size of the number.

  • A price before anyone has looked at the workflow, the files or the systems.
  • A scope written in outcomes ("transform operations") rather than deliverables you could check.
  • No plan for measuring whether it worked, or a baseline nobody recorded.
  • Seats and model usage marked up and resold, with the accounts in the consultant's name.
  • A long monthly commitment as the only way in, before anything has been built or proven.
  • Silence on who owns the prompts, code and configuration when the work is done.

What should you ask before signing?

  1. What exactly is delivered, and how will we know it is finished?
  2. Which pricing model is this, and what happens if the work runs over?
  3. What will the vendor costs be at pilot volume and at a year's expected volume?
  4. Whose name are the accounts, API keys and hosting in?
  5. Who owns the prompts, code and configuration at the end?
  6. What does maintenance look like, and who does it after you leave?
  7. How will we measure whether this saved time or money?

Common questions

Why don't AI consultants publish their prices?

The number depends on team size, workflows in scope, systems to connect, data quality and security review. Many firms, altr included, quote on a call and put the figure in writing before work starts.

Is fixed-fee or hourly better for AI consulting?

Fixed fee is usually better once the workflow, systems and finish line can be written down, because the consultant carries the overrun risk. Hourly suits early, unclear work.

Should I start with a monthly fee?

Generally no. A monthly fee makes sense once something is running for it to cover. Start with a bounded engagement.
