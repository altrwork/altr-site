AI training on your team's real work

We set up Claude for your company and teach your people to use it on the tasks they already do, so they leave with something that already does part of their job, and plain rules on what is safe to put in.

You can book training on its own. Looking for the free public sessions? Those are our Claude workshops for Tampa Bay small businesses.

Two ways in. The difference is depth.

Neither one is a prerequisite for the other.

  • One to four people, hands-on

    For the people who own a process end to end. We work through it at their own keyboard, on real files, and you get a written map of that workflow.

  • Up to ten people, broader

    For getting a group off zero at once. Everyone leaves with prompts and a workflow of their own that already runs.

What the session can cover.

We choose the mix with you before the session.

  • ChatGPT and AI essentials

    A shared method for getting useful output from the tools the team already has.

  • Claude, Cowork and Claude Code

    Claude for everyday work, Cowork for multi-step tasks across files, Claude Code when the work needs a build.

  • Building internal tools

    Turn a process held together by spreadsheets and memory into a tool the team can run.

  • Agents and automation

    Build recurring work into a system without removing the approvals that keep it safe.

  • AI security for teams

    What information stays out, how connected tools can be misused, and where checking is required.

  • AI policy

    Practical rules on ownership, approved tools and review, without turning every decision into a committee.

How a session runs.

Length is set with you on the call. Our public Claude workshops are separate and free.

  • Before

    We collect the real work: the emails, documents, reports and requests your team repeats every week.

  • During

    People work on their own tasks with guidance, so what they build already fits how their work is described.

  • After

    The prompts, workflows and notes stay with your team. Nothing depends on us being in the room again.

Questions before a workshop.

What makes this different from generic AI training?

We run the session against your work, not a demo dataset. Before the workshop we collect the repeated tasks your team actually does, then build the session around those examples. People leave with prompts and workflows they built during the session, in the tools they already use.

How long is a session and how many people can attend?

It runs in one of two formats, and a team can start with either. A working session is hands-on with the one to four people who own a process and returns a written map of that workflow. A team workshop covers up to ten people and is less hands-on with each person. Length is set with you on the call, and teams split it however suits them: one afternoon, two mornings, or weekly blocks while the work is still fresh. Fees are quoted on a call rather than published.

Who should be in the room?

The people who own the work, plus whoever will make decisions about changing it. Mixed rooms of operators and their manager work better than either group alone, because the judgment calls about what to automate get made in the session instead of afterward.

Do people need technical experience?

No. The workshop is about judgment: when to trust output, when to edit, when to escalate, and how to describe work clearly enough that a model can help with it. Those skills matter more than tooling knowledge.

What happens after the workshop?

You keep the prompts, workflows, and operating notes produced during the session. If it surfaces a process that should be systemized rather than done by hand, the next step is a build, quoted as one fixed fee against what the session found. An ongoing advisory retainer is available after that. Both come after enablement rather than instead of it.
