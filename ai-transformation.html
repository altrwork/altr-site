04 / First engagement

What a first engagement delivers.

Most clients start with an audit of one repeated process or a training session for the team. Either one stands on its own, and neither is a prerequisite for the other.

A / Audit

The work, written down.

We sit with the people who do the work and map one process: the documents, the tools, the hand-offs and who checks the result. You get a recommendation either way: train the team, build something, or leave the process alone. If something is worth building, the scope and price are in writing before anything starts.

B / Training

A team that uses it.

Hands-on sessions on your team's real work, either with the one to four people who own a process or a workshop for up to ten. People leave with prompts and workflows they built in the session, and those stay with your team.

We are based in Tampa and work with teams anywhere in the US. Across Tampa Bay we come to you; see AI consulting in Tampa. Everywhere else the same work runs over video. If the process only needs a plain, reliable automation, talk to us as a workflow automation consultant in Tampa instead.

05 / Questions

Questions about AI consulting.

  • What does an AI consulting company do for a small business?

    It finds where AI fits in the work you already do, changes the process around it, and trains the people who do it. At altr that starts with one repeated process and ends with a recommendation: train the team, build a system, or leave the process alone.

  • How is altr different from other AI consulting companies?

    We start with the work, not a tool or a slide deck. Your team keeps the prompts, workflows and documentation. When something needs a build, our engineers carry the plan into an internal system or customer-facing product.

  • Do we need technical staff to work with you?

    No. The training is about judgment: when to trust the output, when to edit it and when to escalate. People work on their own tasks in the tools they already use.

  • How much does AI consulting cost?

    Fees are quoted on a call rather than published. Scope and price are agreed in writing before any work starts.
