Three phases

A useful strategy makes the path smaller and clearer. It identifies the work that matters, tests the assumptions with the team, and gives technology a defined job.

  1. 01

    Map

    We sit with you and your team to understand how work gets done today, then decide how AI moves time, margin, and revenue.

  2. 02

    Prove

    We run the improved workflow on real work with the people who own it, set the guardrails, and document a playbook the team can repeat.

  3. 03

    Deploy

    We build the connector, agent, or internal tool only once the workflow has proven it needs one to run reliably, and your team keeps the prompts and the operating notes.

After launch, some teams keep us on in an advisory retainer. Most do not need to.

How we pick the first workflow.

An AI strategy that starts with a tool list stalls. Ours starts with one workflow, chosen on four tests. A candidate that fails any of them waits.

  1. 01

    It repeats

    It runs every week or every day, so an hour saved is an hour saved many times over.

    Frequency
  2. 02

    Someone can judge the output

    A named person can look at the result and say whether it is right. Without that, nothing downstream can be trusted.

    Review
  3. 03

    The inputs are findable

    The documents, fields, and systems it depends on exist and can be reached, even if they are messy.

    Inputs
  4. 04

    A mistake is recoverable

    A wrong draft costs a correction, not a client. High-stakes work comes later, once the review habit exists.

    Risk
