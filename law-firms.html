What law firms ask about AI first.

How do you handle client confidentiality and privilege?

We define what each workflow may access, which matters are in scope, where information is allowed to travel, and what stays out of the system entirely. Your obligations set the boundaries; we build inside them and write down what was decided.

Does this make legal judgments?

No. These workflows prepare, organize, and draft. The lawyer reviews and owns the work product.

Do we have to replace our practice management system?

No. We build around the systems you already run and the way your firm already files things. Replacing core software is a separate decision and rarely the first useful step.

Where should we start?

Pick one recurring process with clear inputs and a clear output: new client intake, a document type you review constantly, or the billing narrative your team rebuilds every month. A paid team workshop with the people who own that work is one way in.

Do you only work with Florida firms?

No. We are based in Tampa and work with firms anywhere in the US, in person across Tampa Bay and remotely elsewhere. The Florida Bar opinion below applies to Florida lawyers; firms in other states start from ABA Formal Opinion 512 and their own bar's guidance.

Generative AI and lawyer ethics in Florida.

This is not legal advice. The lawyer still owns the work product. We build inside the firm's obligations; we do not replace them. Outside Florida, the national starting point is the ABA's Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024), which is also advisory and covers competence, confidentiality, client communication, supervision, and fees. Your own state bar's rules and opinions govern.

The opinion that applies

The Florida Bar's Board of Governors approved Ethics Opinion 24-1 on January 19, 2024. Advisory ethics opinions are not binding. The opinion says lawyers may use generative AI in the practice of law if they protect client confidentiality, provide accurate and competent services, avoid improper billing, and comply with advertising rules.

Confidentiality and consent

Opinion 24-1 tells lawyers to research a program's policies on data retention, data sharing, and self-learning before putting client information into it. It recommends obtaining the affected client's informed consent before using a third-party generative AI program if that use would disclose confidential information.

The lawyer still owns the work

The opinion is explicit: lawyers remain responsible for their work product and professional judgment. They must develop policies to verify that generative AI use matches their ethical obligations. That is the same line we already take on this page. These workflows prepare, organize, and draft. The lawyer reviews and owns the result.

What we do not do

We do not tell a firm that Opinion 24-1 is a green light to skip review, skip consent, or treat a chatbot as an associate. We map what each workflow may access, where information may travel, and what stays out. Your obligations set the boundaries.
