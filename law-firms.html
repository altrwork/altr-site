Where AI fits

Keep the judgment. Stop rebuilding the process.

The billable work is analysis, strategy, and advice. AI is most useful on the work surrounding it: the intake forms, the document handling, the same summary written a fourth time, and the time entry nobody wants to reconstruct on a Friday.

Client and matter intake

Turn inquiries into structured matter records, conflicts-check inputs, and engagement-ready summaries without retyping the same details across systems.

Document and contract review

Build a repeatable first pass that surfaces clauses, dates, obligations, and deviations from your standard, with the citation back to the source so a lawyer can verify it fast.

Drafting from your own precedent

Time capture and billing narratives

Common questions

What law firms ask first.

How do you handle client confidentiality and privilege?

We define what each workflow may access, which matters are in scope, where information is allowed to travel, and what stays out of the system entirely. Your obligations set the boundaries; we build inside them and write down what was decided.

Does this make legal judgments?

No. These workflows prepare, organize, and draft. The lawyer reviews and owns the work product.

Do we have to replace our practice management system?

No. We build around the systems you already run and the way your firm already files things. Replacing core software is a separate decision and rarely the first useful step.

Where should we start?

Pick one recurring process with clear inputs and a clear output: new client intake, a document type you review constantly, or the billing narrative your team rebuilds every month.

Bring us one process your firm rebuilds every week.

We will tell you whether it needs training, a build, or nothing at all.
