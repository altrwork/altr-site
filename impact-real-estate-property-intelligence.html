CRE Harness: from a property address to a branded BOV.

The Challenge

Commercial real estate teams win assignments with broker opinions of value (BOVs), and a good one takes hours to assemble. Someone pulls the county property record, finds and screens the comparable sales, checks zoning and flood zone, works out who is behind the LLC that owns the building, and then lays it all out in the firm’s template.

Each of those steps lives in a different system, so the same figures get retyped and every document comes out a little different. When an owner asks what their building is worth, the firm that answers first with a credible document has the advantage.

The Solution

altr designed and built CRE Harness, a property research and document system for brokerage teams in the Tampa Bay market. Brokers use it from Claude, through a connector to the county records of Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco and Polk, or from mortr, altr’s own CRM for CRE teams. They start with an address and end with a finished BOV in their firm’s branding.

Key capabilities include:

  1. Finding and screening comparable sales from public record: same use type, a size band either side, market sales only, portfolio deeds removed, with every exclusion stated
  2. Looking up zoning, land use, flood zone and traffic counts from county, city and federal sources
  3. Surfacing owners who may be ready to sell, using holding period, ownership tenure and loan maturities where the debt is on file
  4. Tracing an owning LLC through the Florida corporate registry and SEC Form D filings to the people behind it
  5. Generating a BOV as a PDF in the firm’s logo, colors and typefaces, signed with the broker’s own contact details

The Impact

CRE Harness does the assembly. Pulling the record, screening the sales, chasing the LLC, fetching aerial imagery and laying out the document all happen in one request instead of across separate county, registry and mapping sites.

It does not do the judgment. Condition, tenancy, rents and current competition are in no public record, so the broker still decides where in the range the building sits. Every figure names its source and the date of the county file it came from, so the document can be checked before it reaches an owner.

Because the research and the layout are the same every time, a team’s documents stop varying with whoever built them, and brokers spend the saved time on the owner conversation.
