We build AI connectors for clients. Cloudflare is why it takes days.

Most of what we build for clients is a connector. It is the least glamorous and most useful thing in applied AI: the piece that lets an assistant actually reach a company’s own records. Ask Claude who owns a building, or what a customer ordered last spring, and it can only answer if something handed it the data. That something is an MCP server, and ours run on Cloudflare Workers.

This used to be a project. A server to run it on, a deploy pipeline, monitoring, and somebody whose job is to notice when it stops. Now it is a file you publish — nothing to patch, no capacity to plan for, no region to choose, and live in a few hundred cities the minute it ships. The whole thing sits on the $5 Workers plan, which includes ten million requests a month. That is more headroom than most of our clients will use in a year.

The part that actually changed our pace is that Cloudflare put everything behind an API, so Claude can drive it. Spin up a worker, provision a database, point a domain at it, add a nightly schedule, check what is currently deployed — we describe it, Claude does it, and the infrastructure gets edited in the same conversation as the code. No tab-switching between writing a tool and giving it somewhere to live. It is also all one account: compute, databases, DNS, the domains themselves, outbound email. When a connector needed sign-in links we did not go shopping for a mail provider, we added an email binding.

The client ends up with one connector that behaves differently depending on who opens it, because our servers hold nothing between requests — valuation sees valuation tools, capital markets sees recorded debt, property management sees its own. A new team is one line and a redeploy. Their people install it and sign in through a browser, so there is no token to paste and nothing sensitive sitting in a config file.

Two of us have connectors in production for several clients and almost none of the week goes into keeping them up. That is the whole growth story, honestly. We can say yes to a build, deliver it in days, hand it over with a sign-in link instead of a setup guide, and spend the rest of the week talking to the next client.
