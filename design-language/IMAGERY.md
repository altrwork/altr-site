# Imagery plan: which engraving goes on which screen, and why

Every image on the site is an 18th-century engraving from Diderot's
*Encyclopedie*. This file decides which one goes where. The rule is simple:
**the picture has to be about the same thing the words are about.** An
engraving that is merely handsome is decoration, and decoration is what makes a
site look sporadic.

## The organizing idea

altr sells a sequence: **map the workflow, prove it with the team, deploy what
earns a role.** A building is made the same way, and the Encyclopedie documents
that sequence plate by plate:

| altr does | A building does | The plate shows |
|---|---|---|
| Maps the workflow | Surveys and draws the plan | Floor plans, stone-cutting geometry |
| Proves it with the team | Lays brick, cuts stone, raises the arch | Masonry coursing, vaulting, mason's tools |
| Deploys what earns a role | Stands as a finished elevation | Building facades |

So the imagery is not a set of nice old pictures. It is one continuous argument
about how something durable gets built, and each page borrows the stage of that
argument it is actually making.

This also happens to be the right vocabulary for the audience: the largest
client family is commercial real estate, and these are drawings of buildings.

## The map

### Home

| Section | What the words say | Image | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | "We find the work your team repeats every week, and build the system that handles the rest" | Elevation of the Bibliotheque Royale - a long facade of identical bays | Wash behind the type |
| How we work | Map, prove, deploy | none | The ledger carries it |
| Why teams call us | "making them reliable inside work your team already depends on" | Brick coursing and wall sections | Wash behind the type |

The hero is a finished building whose order comes from one bay repeated. That
is the argument of the headline, in a drawing.

The closing band is brickwork: load-bearing, unglamorous, the thing the
building actually depends on. That is the argument of "reliable inside work
your team already depends on."

## Rationing

One cutout and one wash per page, at most. Around thirty of the fifty pages get
no image at all, and that is deliberate: the plates are the most expensive thing
on the site and the fastest to wear out. A page with no engraving is not an
unfinished page.

## Treatments

**Wash** - behind type, full width. Levelled hard so the line work stays line
work: near-ink darks, clean paper whites, no blur. A paper scrim keeps it opaque
under the copy and opens across the image side.

**Cutout** - beside the copy. Alpha-cut so every pixel lighter than the plate's
own paper is transparent and the drawing carries no ground at all.

Both are built by the scripts described in
[../assets/plates/CREDITS.md](../assets/plates/CREDITS.md), which also records
every source file and its Commons URL.

## Adding one

Before adding an image to a page, answer in one sentence what that page argues.
If the engraving does not show that thing, do not use it - leave the page
without an image. There are more handsome plates than there are pages that
need one.
