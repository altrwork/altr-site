How we work

We bill clients by saying who to bill.

A working session ends. Instead of opening a dashboard, finding the client, starting an invoice, typing the amount, choosing the terms and checking every field, we say what happened: invoice them a thousand dollars for the enablement session. The invoice exists a second later, drafted and priced, with a link to read it.

That is the whole interaction. No forms, no tab hunting, no wondering whether this client already has a record — typed or spoken, one sentence carries everything the invoice needs, and the parts nobody should have to think about are decided underneath it.

The result is that billing stops being a task you schedule. It happens in the thirty seconds after the work finishes, while the details are still in your head, which is also when a client is most ready to pay.

What the sentence decides for you.

Three things that used to be a checklist are now simply true every time.

The client stays one client

It looks the client up by email first and reuses the record if it exists, so a firm you bill monthly builds one history rather than twelve near-duplicates. Nobody has to remember whether they were set up last quarter.

Bank transfer leads, every time

ACH is offered ahead of card, so the payment page opens on bank transfer. On a $1,000 invoice that is $5 instead of $29.30. It is not a setting anyone toggles per invoice; it is just how they go out.

You still read it before it goes.

Drafting is the slow part. Sending is the part worth a pair of eyes.

It cannot send on its own

Every invoice lands as a draft with a review link. You read the number, then send it. Describing an invoice in a sentence should be fast; putting a wrong number in front of a client should stay impossible.

And it checks itself overnight

Subscriptions can drift if a payment notification goes missing. A job runs every night, asks Stripe what each account actually is, and fixes anything that disagrees, so nobody gets locked out of what they paid for.

Built on

  • Stripe invoicing, subscriptions and billing

This is the easiest part of your sales cycle to fix.

Quoting, invoicing and chasing are pure overhead, and they are the first thing worth handing to something you can talk to. We build it around the tools you already bill through.
