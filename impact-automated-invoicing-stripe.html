How we work

How we use Stripe to close out a sale faster.

The slowest part of a small consulting business is not the work. It is everything after the work. The session ends, the invoice does not get written until Friday, the client pays a couple of weeks after that, and a job you finished on the 3rd turns into money on the 25th.

So we made the invoice part instant. A session ends and we just say it: invoice them a thousand dollars for the enablement session. One sentence, typed or spoken. The invoice exists a second later in Stripe, drafted and priced, with a link to read it.

There are two of us and no back office, so the old routine was never hard, just fiddly enough to put off. Find the client, start an invoice, type the amount, pick the terms, check every field, wonder whether this firm is already in there from last quarter. All of that now happens underneath the sentence. It finds the client by email and reuses the record, so a firm we bill monthly keeps one history instead of twelve near duplicates. It puts ACH ahead of card, so the payment page opens on bank transfer. That is $5 in fees on a $1,000 invoice instead of $29.30, every time, without anyone remembering to set it.

It stops at a draft on purpose. The script cannot finalise an invoice and cannot email one. Drafting is the tedious part and should be instant, but sending is where a wrong number reaches a client, so the last click stays ours. There is also a job that runs overnight and checks our records against Stripe in case a payment notification went missing and somebody is sitting in the wrong state. It is boring and it earns its keep about twice a year.

The point of all of it is timing. Billing now happens in the thirty seconds after the work finishes rather than on Friday, so the client gets the invoice while the session is still fresh in their head, which is exactly when they are most ready to pay it. For a two person company that is the difference between a sale that closes and one that drags into next month.
