How we work

Every invoice drafts itself. None of them send by accident.

Billing a client used to mean opening a dashboard, finding or retyping the customer, entering the amount, picking the terms, and checking every field twice. One command now does all of it: it finds the customer by email so a repeat client stays on one record, creates the invoice, attaches the line item, and prints a link to review it.

It stops exactly there. The script cannot finalise an invoice and cannot email one — that is a deliberate limit, not a missing feature. A wrong number sent to a client is the one thing in this process a machine should never be able to do on its own, so the last step is a person reading the invoice and pressing send.

It also opens ACH ahead of card, so the payment page leads with bank transfer. On a $1,000 invoice that is $5 instead of $29.30. Nothing to remember, nothing to set per invoice — the cheaper option is simply the one the client sees first.

The part that runs while nobody is watching.

Stripe tells the platform when a subscription changes. Every so often that message does not arrive.

Why a missed message matters

A webhook is the only thing that writes a customer’s plan status, and Stripe stops retrying after a window. Miss one and a paying firm can be locked out of what it bought, or a cancelled one served forever. Nothing in the system would notice.

So we ask Stripe every night

A job runs at 04:40 and asks Stripe directly what every subscription is, then repairs anything that disagrees. Stripe is the authority; the platform corrects itself against it. It reads, compares, writes only on a difference, and logs every repair.

Careful about what counts as cancelled.

Most billing bugs that reach a customer are a status read too bluntly.

An unpaid first invoice is not a cancellation

A subscription that has not settled yet, or one with collection paused, is treated as past due rather than gone. Reading either as cancelled is exactly how a paying firm loses access. Only a real cancellation and an expired incomplete are terminal.

It never writes back to Stripe

The job creates nothing and cancels nothing. It moves in one direction, which means the worst it can do on a bad night is leave a record untouched for another day.
