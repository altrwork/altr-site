Florida property appraiser data for commercial property

Alex BrittonSeptember 28, 2026

Every Florida county property appraiser publishes the same core facts about every parcel: who owns it, what the county thinks it is worth, what it last sold for. Read carelessly, they mislead.

Short answer

Florida property appraiser data gives you the owner of record, the mailing address, the parcel's use code, building and land characteristics, the county's just value, and a history of recorded sales with qualification codes. It does not carry rents, income, leases, tenants, parking or clear height. For commercial property it supports ownership research and sales comparison, not an income valuation.

What do just value, assessed value and taxable value mean on the tax roll?

Just value is the appraiser's market value estimate as of January 1. Assessed value is just value after any cap on annual increases. Taxable value is assessed value minus exemptions. For commercial property, just value says the most about the market.

Just valueThe appraiser's market value estimate as of January 1Mass appraisal of the market, the building and the landA starting point for value, and comparing sale prices to the roll
Assessed valueJust value limited by assessment capsHomestead caps for residences; a 10 percent annual cap on most non-homestead property for non-school levies, reset on a change of ownershipUnderstanding the tax bill, not the market
Taxable valueAssessed value minus exemptionsHomestead, institutional and other exemptionsEstimating taxes a buyer will inherit or reset

Just value is a mass appraisal, so it is closer on a typical building than an unusual one. In a rising market the roll lags sale prices, so the ratio of recent prices to just value sits above one. Measured across comparable sales, that ratio is one of the most useful numbers the roll gives you.

What do DOR use codes tell you?

The use code is the appraiser's classification of a parcel's predominant use, on a statewide Department of Revenue list. Learn the two-digit ranges: 00 to 09 residential, 10 to 39 commercial, 40 to 49 industrial, 50 to 69 agricultural, 70 to 79 institutional, 80 to 89 governmental, 90 to 99 miscellaneous.

Within them, 39 is hotels and motels and 48 is warehousing and distribution. Many counties add local digits, so a four-digit county code usually begins with the DOR category. Filter on the code as text with leading zeros kept, or vacant land and residential codes mix together.

A use code is not zoning. County zoning layers generally cover unincorporated land only; inside a city, use the city's zoning map.

How do you read the sales file and qualification codes?

Each recorded transfer shows a date, price, instrument or book and page, and a qualification code saying whether the appraiser treated it as an arm's-length sale. Codes 01 and 02 are qualified. Disqualified codes cover related-party transfers, corrective deeds and other non-sales. The Department of Revenue revises the list, so read the version for the sale's date.

Three habits prevent most errors.

  • Treat a $100 deed as a signal, not a price. Florida deeds often recite $10 or $100 when no market sale happened: a transfer into an LLC, between family, or to fix a title. Where the county publishes a qualification code, it wins. Some counties record an unqualified transfer at zero.
  • Watch for portfolio sales. One deed can convey several parcels for one price, shown on every parcel's row. Count the parcels sharing the instrument number. Never sum that price across them or divide it by one building's square footage.
  • Know which file you are reading. In Hillsborough, the last-sale fields on the parcel file hold the last qualified sale, which is not always the last transfer. The full transfer history is published as a separate sales file.

Why do the most recent months look slow?

The data has not arrived yet. The clerk records the deed, the appraiser processes it, and the next file publishes it. In the Tampa-metro county files the trailing 30 to 45 days routinely read low for that reason alone.

Before you tell an owner the market slowed, check the file's publish date and the date of the newest sale in it. A bulk roll can trail the county's own website by days or weeks. Print that date on any document built on sales data.

Who owns it, and do they occupy it?

The roll gives the owner of record and the tax-bill mailing address. For a commercial parcel the owner is usually an LLC, and the mailing address is the first clue: a bill mailed to the property suggests an owner-user, one mailed across the country suggests an investor. Homestead exemptions rarely apply to commercial parcels, so owner occupancy is an inference from the mailing address matching the site address.

To see who is behind an LLC, search the Florida Division of Corporations at Sunbiz. A filing lists officers or managers, registered agent and principal address. The agent is often a service company, so read the officers first. Some owners will not be there: a Delaware statutory trust or out-of-state limited partnership may never register in Florida. Roll names are often abbreviated, so try the name more than one way.

What doesn't the property roll carry?

The roll describes a parcel for taxation, not a building for leasing or financing. No Florida county publishes:

  • Rents, net operating income or operating expenses
  • Leases, tenants, lease expirations or occupancy
  • Parking counts, clear height, dock doors or power
  • Property management

Debt is not on the roll either. Recorded mortgages sit with the county clerk, and each clerk sets its own access terms. Pinellas also publishes hurricane evacuation zones, which affect insurance and therefore value.

What are the statewide NAL and SDF files?

The Department of Revenue collects every county's roll in one format. NAL (name, address, legal) is the real property roll, SDF is sales data, and NAP covers tangible personal property. Counties submit a preliminary roll around July 1 and final rolls after certification, county by county.

Only the latest version of each file is posted on the Department's portal. As of September 2026 earlier rolls are available by request: NAL and NAP back to 2002, sales files back to 2009. Statewide files make it easy to compare counties or fill a field one county's export lacks, such as residential unit count. For a single parcel, the county's own files are more current and detailed.

Common questions

What does just value mean on a Florida tax roll?

On a Florida tax roll, just value is the property appraiser's estimate of a parcel's market value as of January 1 of the tax year. Assessed value is just value after any cap on annual increases, and taxable value is assessed value minus exemptions.

Is the price on a Florida deed always the sale price?

No. Deeds that recite nominal consideration, commonly $10 or $100, usually record transfers that were not market sales, such as a move into an LLC or a family transfer. Check the appraiser's qualification code, and check whether one deed conveyed several parcels for a single price.

What does a DOR use code of 10 to 39 mean?

It means the property appraiser classifies the parcel's predominant use as commercial. Codes 40 to 49 are industrial, 00 to 09 residential, and the rest cover agricultural, institutional, governmental and miscellaneous uses. A use code is not a zoning district.

Does the property appraiser publish rents or leases?

No. Florida property appraisers do not publish rents, income, expenses, leases, tenants, parking or clear height. Those come from the owner, a rent roll, or a broker's own files.

How do I find out who owns a commercial building in Tampa?

Search the address on the county property appraiser's site: Hillsborough for Tampa, Pinellas for St. Petersburg and Clearwater, and Pasco or Polk for parcels north and east of Tampa. The roll shows the owner of record and mailing address. If the owner is an LLC, search its name on Sunbiz, the Florida Division of Corporations site, to see its officers or managers and registered agent.
