AI workflows for commercial real estate teams.

Commercial real estate teams can use AI to organize property research, prepare underwriting inputs, work across approved files and systems, draft review-ready deliverables, and monitor recurring market signals. The right workflow keeps brokers, analysts, asset managers, and principals in control of assumptions and decisions. altr is based in Tampa and works with CRE teams nationwide.

Start with work the team already repeats.

AI creates value in CRE when it has a defined job, approved sources, and a person who owns the output. It should shorten the path from source material to a decision-ready draft - not hide the assumptions behind the decision.

Brokerage and deal pursuit

Organize ownership research, public-record sales, market context, CRM history, and brand standards into a consistent first draft for BOVs, pitches, and owner conversations.

Acquisitions and underwriting

Read OMs and diligence files, identify missing assumptions, prepare model inputs, compare scenarios, and keep an audit trail for the analyst or principal who approves the work.

Asset and property operations

Summarize rent rolls, lease terms, work orders, operating reports, and recurring exceptions from approved systems so asset and property teams can review what changed.

Development and diligence

Structure site research, entitlement documents, consultant reports, diligence checklists, and project-room files without asking a model to make legal, engineering, or investment judgments.

Market and ownership intelligence

Monitor public records, recent sales, ownership changes, debt signals, and local activity on a defined cadence, then route exceptions to the people responsible for acting on them.

Documents and reporting

Turn reviewed research into standardized investment summaries, client materials, internal memos, and recurring reports while preserving source links and company formatting.

A useful AI workflow has five parts.

A prompt is only one part of the system. Reliable work also needs a source boundary, reusable instructions, a place to work, an output standard, and a named reviewer.

01 · Sources

Name the approved OMs, spreadsheets, public records, CRM objects, property systems, and project folders the workflow can use.

02 · Playbook

Package the questions, assumptions, calculations, exceptions, and sequence your strongest team member already follows.

03 · Workspace

Give the model the narrowest practical access to the files and tools needed for this job - never a shared login or an unrestricted machine.

04 · Deliverable

Define what "done" means: a research packet, model input sheet, CRM update, exception report, memo, or branded document.

05 · Review

Assign the broker, analyst, property manager, asset manager, or principal who validates sources and owns the final decision.

Guardrail

Do not automate a process simply because a model can attempt it. Start where errors are visible and review is practical. Keep shared credentials, licensed data your vendor excludes from AI tools, and final investment or legal judgment outside the model.

01 · Skills: package the playbook

Turn underwriting context, required inputs, review steps, and output standards into reusable instructions instead of rewriting the same prompt.

02 · Connectors: reach approved systems

Give AI a controlled route to the CRM, property-management platform, or internal tool when an authorized connection exists.

03 · Cowork: operate on approved files

Build and revise the underwriting model in one connected folder, so the output stays in the spreadsheet the team will review instead of an upload-and-download loop.

Run a weekly recorded-sales digest or pipeline review in the cloud on a cadence, with a clear source, output and person responsible for review.

How can commercial real estate teams use AI?

Commercial real estate teams can use AI to organize property research, prepare underwriting inputs, work across approved files and systems, draft review-ready deliverables, and monitor recurring market signals. The strongest workflows preserve human approval for assumptions, investment decisions, client commitments, and licensed data.

What CRE workflow should a team automate first?

Start with a repeated workflow that has stable inputs, a clear output, and an identifiable reviewer. Property research, CRM updates, recurring market summaries, document intake, and first-pass diligence are usually better starting points than investment decisions or irregular negotiations.

Can AI connect to CoStar?

Not as of September 2026. There is no CoStar connector for Claude, and CoStar's public terms bar sharing passcodes with AI agents, scraping, and using its data as input to generative AI. AI can do the work around CoStar using public records, your own files, and systems with authorized connectors or APIs.

Does AI replace brokers, analysts, or asset managers?

No. A well-designed CRE workflow uses AI to collect inputs, follow repeatable steps, and prepare review-ready work. Brokers, analysts, asset managers, and principals remain responsible for assumptions, source validation, judgment, relationships, and final decisions.

How does altr start an AI project with a CRE team?

We start by mapping one real workflow: its sources, assumptions, handoffs, approvals, exceptions, and final deliverable. The team first learns to run the improved process with AI. Custom software is considered only when the validated workflow needs system access, automation, or deployment.
